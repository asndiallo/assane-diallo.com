# Guides: outlines (PLAN.md D34)

Draft outlines for the guides that sit next to the vertical pages. Nothing here is published.
Each guide gets written from its outline, then approved by Assane (EN and FR) before it ships.
Published: restaurants (`src/content/guides/{en,fr}/restaurants.md`, approved 2026-11-12).

## Rules for every guide

- **Who it's for:** the owner of one kind of business, not other SEOs. Plain words, no
  "GBP", "NAP", "GEO" or "map pack" without saying what it means.
- **What it's for:** a link to send in a first outreach message, and an answer to the
  searches an owner actually types. It is not a pitch: the check-up offer comes once, at the end.
- **Honesty:** no ranking or timeline promises ("it takes months" is said plainly). No
  numbers, client names or results that aren't approved and sourced. No case study gets cited
  until it covers that kind of business with consent.
- **Shape:** 1,200–1,800 words, one `<h1>`, a "Last reviewed" date, linked from its vertical
  page and linking back to it and to `/google-checklist/`. Checked once a year, or sooner when
  Google changes something it covers (like the Q&A removal, D26).
- **Where:** URL `/for/<vertical>/guide/` (FR `/fr/pour/<vertical>/guide/`), so the
  guide stays with its page. Content in a `guides` collection, same key across locales.
- **Measure:** link clicks from outreach, seen in Cloudflare Web Analytics by path. Search
  traffic is a bonus, not the test.

## 1. Restaurants & catering

**Research:** [research/restaurants.md](research/restaurants.md) (sources and live checks,
2026-11-12) · **Links from:** `/for/restaurants/` · **Working title:** "Getting your restaurant picked on
Google, when people are already hungry"

**Searches it answers:** restaurant near me · [dish] near me · [cuisine] restaurant [city] ·
catering [city] · open now · how to add a menu to Google

**Sections**

1. **How people pick a restaurant on a phone.** The map results, the "open now" filter,
   photos, menu and reviews. The decision takes seconds, and most of it happens on the
   listing, not the website.
2. **Fix these first** (from the checklist): owner access to the profile, correct hours, a
   phone that gets answered, an order or menu link that works on a phone, real photos.
3. **Your menu.** Add it to the profile with prices. Name dishes the way customers type
   them (example: jollof rice, thieboudienne, injera), not only in house shorthand.
4. **Photos.** Real dishes, the front door from the street (so people find you), the
   inside. Add new ones each month; no stock photos.
5. **Hours.** Holiday and special hours. Wrong hours drop you out of "open now" and send
   people to a closed door, which turns into bad reviews.
6. **Ordering and catering.** A direct order link next to the delivery apps, and catering
   listed as its own service (with its own page if it's a real part of the business).
7. **Categories.** One primary category that matches the food; add "caterer" etc. only if
   true.
8. **Reviews.** Ask at the right moment (QR code on the table or receipt), reply to every
   one, including bad ones. No discounts for reviews and no asking only happy customers:
   Google's rules forbid both.
9. **ChatGPT and other AI assistants.** Their answers draw on the same listing, reviews and
   website. What you can control (accurate, complete, consistent info) and what nobody can
   (what an AI says).
10. **What takes time.** What you can fix in an afternoon vs. what builds over months.
11. **End:** link to the checklist, then the free 15-minute check-up.

**Decided (2026-11-12)**

- Audience: all restaurants, with African dishes as the examples (D24 allows the focus on
  this page; the guide stays useful to every restaurant prospect).
- No restaurant case study yet; cite none.

## 2. Home services (HVAC, plumbing, cleaning, handyman)

**Links from:** `/for/home-services/` · **Working title:** "Getting the call when something
breaks: Google for home service pros"

**Searches it answers:** plumber near me · emergency plumber · AC repair [city] · HVAC
[city] · water heater installation [city] · how to set a service area on Google

**Sections**

1. **How emergency searches work.** People call one of the first few names and hire
   whoever answers. Being on the list matters, and so does picking up.
2. **Fix these first** (from the checklist): owner access, hours, a phone that gets
   answered, a call or quote button, real photos.
3. **Service area, done right.** If customers don't come to you, hide the address and set
   the area (Google's rule). List only the cities you really cover; stretching it gets
   the profile flagged and wastes calls.
4. **Services and categories.** The primary category that matches the main trade
   (plumber, HVAC contractor); each service listed with a plain description.
5. **Hours and emergency calls.** Say 24/7 only if someone answers at 2 a.m. Missed calls
   are lost jobs; a callback text helps.
6. **Photos of real jobs.** Before/after, the truck, the team. No stock photos.
7. **Reviews after every job.** Text the link the same day. Customers mentioning the job and
   the town helps people reading them; you never write them yourself. Reply to every one.
8. **One page per real service, per real city.** Useful pages, not copies with the city
   name swapped (Google treats those as spam).
9. **Licence and insurance.** If your state licenses your trade, show the number: customers
   check it.
10. **Paid leads vs. your own listing.** Lead sites and Google's paid "Local Services" ads
    are separate products; your free profile is what stays yours. Neutral, no claims about
    their results.
11. **ChatGPT and other AI assistants.** Same point as the restaurant guide, with home
    service questions ("who fixes AC in [city]?").
12. **What takes time,** then the checklist and the free check-up.

**Decided (2026-11-12)**

- One guide for all home services now; split into HVAC and plumbing only if outreach shows a
  need.
- Section 9: keep it to "show it if you have one". Checking a client's licence is the
  separate D23 question for held verticals.
