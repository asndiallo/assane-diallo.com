## Isa's African Hair Braiding (published case study, consent: true)

Usable as published, always with the disclosure "Isa, the owner, is my cousin" and the note
that there's no "before" number (the profile had never been claimed):

- Highlight: "450 calls from her Google profile in 3 months." (source: Business Profile, 3
  months to Oct 2026)
- Top searches that showed the profile: "african hair braiding" (402), "african hair braiding
  savannah ga" (324), "african braiding near me" (192).
- What was done: claimed the profile, fixed hours and address, real photos, a page for each
  main style, a free booking request form that messages the owner.

## Live checks (2026-11-12, searcher near San Antonio, TX)

"african hair braiding san antonio" and "knotless braids near me":

- **No ads at all**, unlike plumbing, HVAC and brakes. The map list comes first.
- All six listings were categorized **"Hair salon"**; none showed a braiding-specific category.
- Every listing had a **"Schedule"** button (online booking connected to the profile).
- Ratings 3.8–4.9 with 85–256 reviews; one salon had "3+ years in business".
- Review lines **matched the style searched**: "my knotless braids", "my small knotless
  braids", "finished my box braids in 4 hours and they provided the hair too", "very calm and
  quite quick".
- "Have AI get prices" button.
- Web results: Booksy and StyleSeat pages (with prices and open appointments), Yelp "Top 10",
  **Instagram braider accounts** (one with 46K followers, "now booking for November and
  December, link to book"), salon websites whose snippets show **prices** ("Knotless Braids
  $200.00 & Up", "Bob Box $180.00"), Facebook pages with "deposit required before
  appointment", and Facebook group threads asking for braider recommendations and "licensed
  braiders".

## Plan for the guide

1. Clients search by style: one service (and one website page) per style, with price from,
   time, and whether hair is included.
2. Address: the signage rule; salon suites; home braiders; mobile braiders (service area); if
   you don't publish the address, say how clients get it (ChatGPT flagged it).
3. Hours vs. appointment only (Google's rule).
4. Category: "Hair salon" is what listings showed; pick the most specific one the dashboard
   offers.
5. Booking: connect a provider so the Schedule button shows, or add your own link; deposits
   and cancellation policy written plainly.
6. Photos: each style, finished look, parts close-up; ask the client's permission.
7. Reviews: style names and "gentle" come from clients' own words; Google rules + FTC
   (relatives, especially in family salons).
8. Instagram, Booksy/StyleSeat, Yelp, Facebook groups: same name, phone and prices
   everywhere; link Instagram to the site and profile.
9. Isa's example, with disclosure.
10. Licence: if your state licenses braiding, show it (no claim about which states).
