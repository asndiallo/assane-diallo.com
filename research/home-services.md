## Live checks (2026-11-12, desktop Chrome at phone width, searcher near San Antonio, TX)

Searches: "plumber san antonio", "ac repair san antonio", "emergency plumber near me", and AI
Mode "my AC stopped working, who can fix it today in san antonio".

- **Two layers of ads before the free listings.** First a "Sponsored" block of paid listings
  (Local Services Ads): rating and review count (10K–17K reviews for the big firms), years in
  business, "Serves <area>", "Open 24 hours", "Family owned" / "Free estimate", and buttons
  Get quote, Book online, Get phone number. Then text ads. The free map list ("Businesses")
  comes after, and its first slot was also an ad: a company about 50 miles away, categorized
  as an HVAC contractor, showing on a plumber search.
- **The free list.** Three businesses with rating, reviews, years in business, city, hours,
  phone, and a review quote about speed: "fixed in under an hour", "help the same day",
  "fixed my AC in less than 24 hrs". One had a "Schedule" button (online booking). On AC repair,
  a company with **138 reviews and no years shown** sat next to companies with 582 and 4.4K:
  small businesses do make the list.
- **Categories** shown: Plumber; HVAC contractor; Air conditioning repair service. Some AC
  companies use the more specific "Air conditioning repair service".
- **"Emergency plumber near me"** showed no map list: one business panel instead, with its
  247 reviews, "Open 24 hours", and its **services list from the profile** (repiping, slab leak
  repair, drain cleaning, water heater…). Filled-in services are what Google shows.
- **Web results** below: Yelp "Top 10" pages, Thumbtack lists, Reddit threads ("any
  recommendations for AC repair?"), and a local newspaper's "best HVAC companies" page. People
  read those, and AI answers quote them.
- **AI buttons.** The plumber search showed "Have AI get prices"; AI Mode answered the AC
  question with "Check appointments for me: get availability and pricing from local businesses,
  then book the right fit". Google may call you on a customer's behalf.

## ChatGPT (owner's account, free plan, screenshots 2026-11-12, near San Antonio)

**"AC repair near me"**

- ChatGPT **assumed car A/C**, saying so, and listed auto repair and tire shops; it later asked
  about the owner's own car by model. Answers are personalized from what ChatGPT remembers,
  and "AC repair" alone is ambiguous. Home AC companies should write "home AC", "central air",
  "HVAC" on their site and profiles.
- Under the answer: **an ad** ("This might also interest you"), from **Angi**, "AC Install
  Pros: Get quotes in minutes", labelled "Ad". ChatGPT shows ads, and marketplaces buy them.
- Price ranges came from a repair shop's website (cited by name).

**"emergency plumber near me"**

- Five plumbers with rating, review count and distance, then three more "worth calling". The
  first had 4.9 / 644 reviews, against 646 on Google the same day: the counts track Google's
  closely here (the restaurant check showed Yelp's ratings; sources vary by answer).
- Each line repeats **what the company's own website says**, with the website cited:
  "emergency calls and texts accepted around the clock", "advertises immediate emergency
  dispatch", "24/7 emergency service; advertises a 5% active-duty military discount",
  "licensed emergency plumbers and round-the-clock phone answering", "listed as operating 24
  hours daily".
- It ended with advice for the customer: shut off the main water valve, and **ask three
  questions before hiring**: can you dispatch now, what's the emergency service-call fee, will
  you give a written estimate.
- The first plumber also appeared in Google's free list for "plumber san antonio".

## Changes to the outline (GUIDES.md §2)

1. **Ads first, honestly:** explain the paid block (Local Services Ads, now inside Google Ads,
   pay per lead, "Google Verified" badge) and the text ads above the free list. The free
   profile is still the base: Ads setup pulls from it. No cost figures, no advice to buy or
   not.
2. **Service area section:** hide the address if customers don't come to you (Google's own
   plumber example), up to 20 areas, within about 2 hours' drive, cities you really cover.
3. **Services list:** fill it in with descriptions; Google shows it in the business panel.
4. **Opening date:** add it, so "years in business" is right.
5. **Hours and emergencies:** "Open 24 hours" appears on the ads and listings people compare
   with; only claim it if someone answers. New: Google may call you for prices on a customer's
   behalf, so answer the phone, keep prices and services on the profile, and know where the
   opt-out is (verified profiles).
6. **Reviews:** the quotes Google shows are about speed and the job. Ask the same day; never
   script. Big competitors have thousands of reviews, but a company with 138 still made the
   list: recent, good reviews count.
7. **Fake competitors:** how to report a listing with a stuffed name or no real address (Maps
   steps + Business Redressal Complaint form). Our own name stays the real one.
8. **Beyond Google:** Yelp (feeds ChatGPT), Angi and Thumbtack (inside ChatGPT, reported),
   Reddit and local "best of" lists. Answer where you're mentioned; never post fake
   recommendations.
9. **From ChatGPT:** say "home" AC/HVAC plainly; put on the website, in plain words, what
   ChatGPT quotes and tells customers to ask: whether you take emergency calls and texts and
   when, how fast you can come, the service-call fee, written estimates, licence and
   discounts. ChatGPT shows ads (an Angi ad appeared).
10. **Licence:** keep "show your licence number if your state licenses your trade" as advice
    (no source needed; no claim about Google using it).
