## Changes to the outline (GUIDES.md §1)

1. **Hours section:** add the restaurant rule (dine-in hours first; takeout and delivery in
   "More hours"), and special hours for holidays, set early and confirmed even when unchanged.
2. **Menu section:** full menu in the menu editor (not a few "popular items"); add your own
   dish names and photos in the Maps app, because yours take precedence over customers'. Also
   put the menu on your website as text, not only a PDF or photo (my advice, not a Google rule:
   say it as advice).
3. **Ordering section:** the Order button sends people to links, so set your own order link
   and a preferred provider. New: Ask Maps can take orders for Square and Toast restaurants (U.S.,
   Aug 2026). Say "if you use Square or Toast, check it's switched on", no more.
4. **New section: "Beyond Google".** Yelp feeds ChatGPT (reviews, photos, details, and
   bookings), and Apple Maps is now used by more than a quarter of people reading reviews. Claim
   both free listings and keep them matching Google.
5. **Reviews section:** use BrightLocal's numbers on recency, star ratings, 20 reviews and
   replies (one survey, dated, all local businesses). No incentives, no picking only happy
   customers.
6. **AI section:** Ask Maps (Google) and ChatGPT (via Yelp and web listings) both read what's
   already out there. Nobody controls what they say; accurate, complete, matching listings and
   recent reviews are what you can do.
7. **Drop** any claim about where ChatGPT gets its data beyond what OpenAI and Yelp say.

## Live checks (2026-11-12, searcher near San Antonio, TX)

Businesses are not named here: these notes go into a public repo. "A", "B"… are real restaurants.

**Google Search** ("jollof rice san antonio", "african restaurant houston", "senegalese
restaurant new york"):

- The three-place list leads every search. Each line shows rating, review count, price range,
  category, open/closed, and one line of evidence for the search: either a **"Menu highlight:
  jollof rice"** (taken from the menu) or a **review quote containing the searched words**
  ("…amazing jollof rice…", "…dreamed I was in Senegal"). Menus and customers' own words are
  what Google quotes back to the searcher.
- **Categories:** "African" and "West African" both show as categories. The Senegalese places
  in New York all show "West African", which suggests there is no "Senegalese restaurant"
  category; the guide says to pick the most specific one the dashboard offers.
- **Location of the searcher matters:** "african restaurant houston" put a San Antonio
  restaurant first for this searcher (about 200 miles from Houston). One search can't show why;
  the guide only says results depend on who is searching and where, so ranking screenshots
  from your own phone aren't the whole picture.
- **Profile buttons** for restaurant A: Order pickup, Order delivery, Website, Directions,
  Save, Share, Call, plus Menu / Reviews / Photos tabs. "Order pickup" opens a provider list
  where the restaurant's own choice (Square) is tagged **"Preferred by business"** and **"No
  fee"**. Directly under the buttons sat a **sponsored DoorDash ad inside the restaurant's own
  profile**. A restaurant without its own order link leaves that space to the apps.
- Filters on top: Open now, Top rated, Accepts reservations, Recently opened, price. Wrong
  hours or a missing reservations link drop you out of a filtered list.

**Google AI Mode** ("where can I get good jollof rice in san antonio"): named the same three
restaurants as the Google list, in the same order. Google's AI answer follows the Google
profile data.

**ChatGPT** (owner's account, "jollof rice near me", screenshots 2026-11-12): different list
from Google.

- Its **"top pick"** was a restaurant its own map card labels **"Permanently closed"**. The
  answer text (address, phone, "jollof rice from $14.99", menu link) came from the restaurant's
  website and a directory, which still look open. A closed business that leaves its website
  and listings up keeps getting sent customers; an open one whose listings look stale risks
  the reverse.
- Ratings on the map cards carry the **Yelp** mark: restaurant A shows **4.0 on Yelp** in
  ChatGPT vs **4.9 (456 reviews) on Google**. Matches Yelp's statement that ChatGPT shows its
  ratings. Your Yelp reviews are what ChatGPT users see.
- Cited sources besides the restaurants' sites: a **local news site** (an opening story) and
  an **African-restaurant directory**. Local press and niche directories get read.
- Prices and dishes in the answer came from a **menu on the website**, as text.

## Changes from the live checks

8. **Menu section:** Google quotes menu items back ("Menu highlight"), and ChatGPT quoted a
   price from a website menu. Both read text: put the menu in Google's menu editor and as text
   on the website.
9. **Ordering section:** show what the buttons look like; your own link gets "Preferred by
   business"; without one, the apps fill the space (a delivery ad even appeared inside a
   profile).
10. **Reviews section:** Google quotes reviews that use the searched words. Ask for reviews;
    never script them (customers' own words are both the rule and what works).
11. **Beyond Google:** Yelp's rating is the one ChatGPT shows, and can differ a lot from
    Google's. Claim and answer on Yelp too.
12. **Closing or moving:** mark it on Google, Yelp, Apple and the website the same day; AI
    answers keep sending people to whatever the listings still say.
13. **Getting mentioned:** local news and niche directories show up as sources. A short
    section on getting listed in relevant directories and telling local press about openings
    or events (no promises).
