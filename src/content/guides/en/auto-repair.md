## What drivers see when they search

Search "auto repair" with the name of your city, on your phone. On the searches I checked, Google showed a map with three shops first, sometimes after an ad. Each one showed the rating, the number of reviews, years in business, the type of shop, the hours, the phone number, and one line from a review. Those lines were about trust and speed: a "reliable mechanic", a car "repaired within an hour".

The good news for an independent shop: the shops in that list had **64, 111 and 121 reviews**. In plumbing or air conditioning, the top names often have thousands. Here, a shop with a hundred honest reviews and a complete profile is in the running.

For specific repairs, the page changes. "Brake repair near me" brought a block of paid listings first, then a map list where the shops used the narrower "Brake shop" category and the review lines named the job: "front and rear brake job", "new brake line within 20 minutes".

Google says it ranks the free listings on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance, and prominence (how well known you are, including reviews and links to your site). You [can't pay for a better place](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) in them.

## Fix these five things first

1. **Own your profile.** Claim and verify it on Google Business Profile. If a former partner or an old marketing company controls it, request ownership.
2. **Hours that are right every day,** including Saturdays and holidays.
3. **A phone that gets answered** during those hours. A driver with a dead car calls the next shop.
4. **Your repairs and the makes you work on, listed.**
5. **Real photos:** the outside of the shop and the sign, the bays, your team at work.

The [free Google checklist](/google-checklist/) goes through the rest, starting with what matters most.

## List the repairs you do, and the cars you work on

When I asked ChatGPT for "auto repair near me", each shop it listed came with one line on what it does: "independent mechanic, general repairs and maintenance", "full-service repairs, A/C, cooling systems", "tires, brakes, batteries, alignments". Then it asked three questions: which vehicle, what repair (check-engine light, brakes, transmission, A/C, tires, inspection…), and what matters most (affordable and trustworthy, quality, or speed). It promised to find "the best shop for the actual problem, not just the closest one".

So tell Google, and your website, exactly what you fix:

- **Your services, one by one,** in [your profile's services list](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455399?hl=en), with a short description and a starting price where you can: "Check-engine light diagnosis", "Brake pads and rotors", "A/C recharge and leak repair", "Transmission service", "State inspection" (only if you're a licensed station).
- **The makes you work on, and the ones you don't.** "We work on Toyota, Honda, Ford and Chevy; no European cars" saves everyone a phone call.
- **Use drivers' words:** "check engine light", "car won't start", "AC blowing warm", not only trade terms.

## Category, name and years in business

- **Main category:** "Auto repair shop" or "Mechanic" for a general shop. Add narrower categories only for work you really do, like "Brake shop" or "Tire shop". Google asks for [as few categories as possible, as specific as possible](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en), and not used as keywords.
- **Name:** exactly as it appears on your sign. Adding "Best Cheap Brakes" to the name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
- **Opening date:** add it. Google may use it to show [how many years you've been in business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10515606?hl=en), right under your name. For a mechanic, years in business is trust.

## Show why you can be trusted

The first web result under my search was a Reddit thread titled "looking for a trustworthy mechanic". Drivers are afraid of being overcharged, and they look for proof before they call. Put what's true on your profile and your website:

- **Certifications you really hold,** like ASE certification or AAA Approved status. Never claim one you don't have.
- **Your warranty** on parts and labor, in plain words: how long, and what it covers.
- **How you price:** a written estimate before any work, a call before anything extra, the old parts shown or returned if the customer wants them.
- **Your diagnostic fee,** and whether it's applied to the repair.
- **If you run a special,** say exactly what it includes. "What does a $99 brake special include?" was one of the questions Google showed for brake searches.

## Hours, Google calling you, and mobile mechanics

- **Holidays:** set [special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) ahead of time.
- **Google may call you for a customer.** For "auto repair" Google showed a "Have AI get prices" button: it can [call or text shops](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16190256?hl=en) to ask about prices and availability for the customer. The caller says it's automated. Answer it like a customer. A verified profile can turn these calls off in Business Profile settings, under "Google automated calls and text messages".
- **Mobile mechanics:** if customers don't come to you, Google wants you to [hide the address and set a service area](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) instead (up to 20 cities or ZIP codes, within about two hours' drive). If you have a shop and also do roadside or at-home work, you can show both. A mobile brake service with 293 reviews and no address sat in the map list I saw.

## Photos

Google asks for photos that are [in focus, well lit, and not heavily filtered](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en), and a photo of the outside helps people find you.

- The front of the shop and your sign, from the street.
- Clean bays and your equipment.
- Your team at work.
- Before and after, when a repair shows: a dented panel, worn pads next to new ones.

Only your own photos, never stock images.

## Reviews: ask after every job

In [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 US adults, about local businesses in general, 74% look for reviews from the last three months, 68% only choose businesses with four stars or more, and 89% expect the owner to reply.

**How to ask.** Text your Google review link when the customer picks up the car, or put a card with a QR code on the counter. Ask every customer.

**What not to do.** Two sets of rules apply:

- [Google's rules](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) forbid offering anything in exchange for a review, like a discount on the next oil change, and asking only the customers you know are happy.
- The FTC's [rule on reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), in effect since October 2024, bans fake reviews (including ones written by AI), paying for positive reviews, undisclosed reviews from employees, and threats to get a bad review removed. It also limits owners and managers asking their relatives for reviews. Courts can fine businesses that break it knowingly.

Never write reviews for customers. Their own words are what Google shows: a review that says "front and rear brake job, car runs great" is the line the next driver reads.

**How to reply.** Short, personal, about the car and the job. For a bad review: thank them, explain what happened without blaming, say what you'll do, and offer to talk. Never share a customer's details.

## Beyond Google: Yelp, Apple Maps, AI assistants and neighbors

- **Yelp:** its "Best 10" list ranked on the first page, and Yelp says ChatGPT shows its [reviews, ratings, photos and business details](https://blog.yelp.com/news/yelp-chatgpt-integration/). Claim your page and keep it matching Google.
- **Apple Maps** is the maps app on every iPhone. [Apple Business Connect](https://businessconnect.apple.com/) is free and lets you control your listing.
- **Directories that check shops,** like AAA's list of approved repair facilities, ranked on the first page too. If you qualify, they're worth it.
- **Neighbors:** Reddit and Facebook threads asking for "a trustworthy mechanic" rank high. You can't make anyone recommend you, and you should never post fake recommendations; honest work gets mentioned.
- **AI answers:** in BrightLocal's survey, 45% had used ChatGPT or another AI tool to find a local business. ChatGPT also shows ads now: under my answer sat one from a tools retailer. Nobody can promise what an AI will say about you; make sure everything it reads is accurate, complete and the same everywhere.

## Your website, kept simple

You don't need a fancy site. You need one that answers what a worried driver asks, on a phone:

- a phone number people can tap to call, at the top of every page;
- your repairs, one by one, and the makes you work on;
- your hours, address and a way to book or ask for a quote;
- your warranty, how estimates work, and your diagnostic fee;
- certifications you really hold;
- your trade and city in the page title, for example "Auto repair in San Antonio".

## What takes an afternoon, and what takes months

In an afternoon: claim your profile, fix hours, list your repairs and makes, add the opening date, upload real photos, and claim Yelp and Apple Business Connect.

Over the following months: a review after every job, a reply to each one, new photos, and being mentioned where drivers ask for a mechanic. Google takes time to reflect this. It usually takes months, and nobody can honestly guarantee where you'll rank.
