## What clients see when they search

> **Do this**
>
> - Search the style you're best known for and your city on your phone, and look at what comes up.

Search "knotless braids" or "African hair braiding" with the name of your city, on your phone. Google shows a map with three salons. Each one shows the rating, the number of reviews, years in business, the hours, the phone number, a **Schedule** button to book, and one line from a review.

That review line matches the style searched. Clients search by style, and Google answers with your clients' own words about that style. A braider with steady, recent reviews and a complete profile is in the running.

Google says it ranks these results on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance, and prominence (how well known you are, including reviews and links to your site). You can't pay for a better place in them.[^rank]

> **What I saw**
>
> On the searches I checked, there were no ads at all: the map came first. The review lines read "I love my small knotless braids", "they finished my box braids in 4 hours and they provided the hair too". The salons in the list had between 85 and 256 reviews, and ratings from 3.8 to 4.9.

## Fix these five things first

> **Do this**
>
> - Claim and verify your Google Business Profile. If someone else set it up years ago, request ownership.
> - List your styles one by one, with a starting price.
> - Set up a way to book from your profile (more below).
> - Make sure the phone gets answered, or messages get a quick reply.
> - Post real photos of your work, style by style.

The [free Google checklist](/google-checklist/) goes through the rest, starting with what matters most.

## One service per style, with a price

> **Do this**
>
> - List every style you do as its own service on your profile and your website, named the way clients search it.
> - Give each a starting price, and say what changes it: size, length, hair included or not.
> - Say how long each style takes.
> - Write your deposit and cancellation policy in plain words.

Add each style to your profile's services list:[^services] knotless braids, box braids, cornrows, Senegalese twists, Fulani braids, boho braids, men's braids, locs, crochet. Knowing how long it takes lets clients plan their day. Prices that are clear before the appointment mean fewer surprises in the chair, and fewer bad reviews.

> **What I saw**
>
> When I asked ChatGPT for "braiding salons near me", one salon was described by its own published prices: box braids, Senegalese twists, kinky twists, "published prices start around $125", with a link to pricing and booking. Then ChatGPT asked three things: which style, what budget, and what matters most. It offered to narrow the list to salons that "actually specialize in your preferred style rather than just general hair salons". Google's results showed prices too, straight from salon websites: "Knotless braids $200 & up".

## Your address: salon, suite, home or mobile

> **Do this**
>
> - In a salon or a suite with your name on it, show the address with the suite number.
> - If you go to clients' homes, hide the address and set a service area instead.
> - If you braid at home and don't publish the address, say clearly how clients get it, for example in the booking confirmation.

Where you braid changes how your profile should look.

- **A salon or a suite:** Google expects any business that shows its address to have a permanent sign with its name at that address.[^guidelines]
- **Braiders working inside someone else's salon:** Google allows a profile for an individual practitioner with their own clients, named with just their name, separate from the salon's own profile.[^guidelines]
- **Going to clients:** a service area can list up to 20 cities or ZIP codes.[^areas]
- **Braiding at home:** think it through. Google's rule about a sign applies to any address you show, and many braiders would rather not publish their home address.

> **What I saw**
>
> When I asked ChatGPT, its first pick was a braider whose "exact street address isn't publicly confirmed": still first, but with a doubt attached.

## Hours and booking

> **Do this**
>
> - If you have set hours when clients can come or book, list them, and set special hours for holidays.
> - Connect a booking provider to your profile (Business Profile → Bookings → choose your provider), or add your own booking link.

Google's guidelines say businesses that work **only by appointment** shouldn't list regular hours.[^guidelines] Holidays go in special hours.[^special] Once you connect a booking provider, Google says it shows up on your profile within a week.[^booking]

Booking doesn't have to cost a monthly fee. For Isa's salon (below), I set up a simple booking request form that sends her a message for each request.

> **What I saw**
>
> Every salon in the map list I saw had a Schedule button.

## Photos of your work

> **Do this**
>
> - Photograph each style you want to be booked for, finished, from the front, back and side.
> - Take close-ups of parts and edges.
> - Photograph your chair, your space and the outside, so clients know where they're going.
> - Add new photos every week you can.
> - Always ask your client's permission before posting a photo of them, and crop out faces if they prefer.

Clients book braids with their eyes. Google asks for photos that are in focus, well lit, and not heavily filtered.[^photos]

> **What I saw**
>
> When ChatGPT described one salon, it was "customers praise clean parts and consistent braiding".

## Category and name

> **Do this**
>
> - Pick the most specific category the dashboard offers for what you do, and add others only if they're true.
> - Use your name exactly as it appears on your sign, your Instagram and your website.
> - If your state requires a licence for braiding, show it on your website.

Google asks for as few categories as possible, as specific as possible.[^guidelines] Adding "Best Knotless Braids Cheap" to the name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.

> **What I saw**
>
> Every salon in the lists I saw used "Hair salon". "Licensed braiders" came up in local Facebook groups: clients ask.

## Reviews: clients describe what matters

> **Do this**
>
> - When the client is happy in the mirror, text your Google review link or show a QR code.
> - Ask every client.
> - Reply to every review: short, personal, about their style.

In BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, about local businesses in general, 74% look for reviews from the last three months, 68% only choose businesses with four stars or more, and 89% expect the owner to reply.[^brightlocal]

Clients' own words decide a lot here.

**What not to do.**

- Google's rules forbid offering anything in exchange for a review, like a discount on the next set, and asking only the clients you know are happy.[^policy]
- The FTC's rule on reviews bans fake reviews (including AI-written ones), paying for positive reviews, and undisclosed reviews from people who work for you. It also limits owners asking their relatives for reviews. In a family salon, that matters.[^ftc]
- Never write reviews for clients, or tell them what to say.

**How to reply.** For a bad review: thank them, own what went wrong, say what you'll do, and offer to talk. Never share a client's details.

> **What I saw**
>
> ChatGPT summed up salons from their reviews: "gentle braiding, patience and affordability". Among the things it asked a client to choose from was "gentle braiding (not too tight)". Google showed lines like "very calm and quite quick with braiding".

## Instagram, booking apps and everywhere else

> **Do this**
>
> - Put the same name, phone and booking link on Instagram, your Google profile and your website, and link them to each other.
> - If you use Booksy, StyleSeat or Yelp, keep your prices and hours the same as on Google.
> - Claim your listing on Apple Business Connect.
> - Never post fake recommendations in Facebook groups; happy clients will tag you.

- **Yelp:** Yelp says ChatGPT shows its reviews, ratings, photos and business details.[^yelp]
- **Apple Maps** is the maps app on every iPhone. Apple Business Connect is free and lets you control your listing.[^apple]
- **AI answers:** in BrightLocal's survey, 45% had used ChatGPT or another AI tool to find a local business.[^brightlocal] Nobody can promise what an AI will say about you; make sure everything it reads is accurate, complete and the same everywhere.

> **What I saw**
>
> Braider accounts on Instagram ranked on Google's first page, one with tens of thousands of followers and a booking link. Booksy, StyleSeat and Yelp pages ranked there too, with prices and open appointments. Local Facebook groups asking "who does affordable knotless braids?" ranked high. Under my ChatGPT answer sat an ad for a hair product: ChatGPT shows ads now.

## What it looked like for one salon

Isa's African Hair Braiding in Savannah, Georgia. Disclosure: Isa, the owner, is my cousin. Her Google profile had never been claimed. I claimed it, fixed the hours and address, added real photos, built a website with a page for each main style, and set up the free booking request form. The result: 450 calls from her Google profile in 3 months (to October 2026). Because the profile had never been claimed, there's no "before" number to compare with: this is a total, not a before-and-after. Her top search was simply "african hair braiding". The [full case study](/work/isa-african-hair-braiding/) has the details and sources.

## Your website, kept simple

> **Do this**
>
> - Make a page for each style, with photos, a starting price, the time it takes and whether hair is included.
> - Add a big "Book" button and a phone number people can tap.
> - Write your deposit and cancellation policy.
> - Show your address, or how clients get it.
> - Put the style and your city in the page title, for example "Knotless braids in San Antonio".

You don't need a fancy site. You need one that answers what a client asks before booking, on a phone.

## What takes an afternoon, and what takes months

In an afternoon: claim your profile, list your styles with prices, connect booking, upload photos of your work, and line up Instagram, Booksy or Yelp with the same details.

Over the following months: a review after every appointment, a reply to each one, new photos every week, and clients tagging you where others ask for a braider. Google takes time to reflect this. It usually takes months, and nobody can honestly guarantee where you'll rank.
