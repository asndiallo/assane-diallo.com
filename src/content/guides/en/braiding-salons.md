## What clients see when they search

Search "knotless braids" or "African hair braiding" with the name of your city, on your phone. On the searches I checked, there were no ads at all: Google showed a map with three salons right away. Each one showed the rating, the number of reviews, years in business, the hours, the phone number, a **Schedule** button to book, and one line from a review.

That review line matched the style searched: "I love my small knotless braids", "they finished my box braids in 4 hours and they provided the hair too". Clients search by style, and Google answers with your clients' own words about that style.

The salons in that list had between 85 and 256 reviews, and ratings from 3.8 to 4.9. A braider with steady, recent reviews and a complete profile is in the running.

Google says it ranks these results on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance, and prominence (how well known you are, including reviews and links to your site). You [can't pay for a better place](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) in them.

## Fix these five things first

1. **Own your profile.** Claim and verify it on Google Business Profile. If someone else set it up years ago, request ownership.
2. **Your styles, listed one by one, with a starting price.**
3. **A way to book from your profile** (more below).
4. **A phone that gets answered,** or a quick reply to messages.
5. **Real photos of your work,** style by style.

The [free Google checklist](/google-checklist/) goes through the rest, starting with what matters most.

## One service per style, with a price

When I asked ChatGPT for "braiding salons near me", one salon was described by its own published prices: box braids, Senegalese twists, kinky twists, "published prices start around $125", with a link to pricing and booking. Then ChatGPT asked three things: which style, what budget, and what matters most. It offered to narrow the list to salons that "actually specialize in your preferred style rather than just general hair salons". Google's results showed prices too, straight from salon websites: "Knotless braids $200 & up".

So list every style you do, as its own service in [your profile's services list](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455399?hl=en), and on your website:

- **The style name the way clients search it:** knotless braids, box braids, cornrows, Senegalese twists, Fulani braids, boho braids, men's braids, locs, crochet.
- **A starting price,** and what changes it: size, length, hair included or not.
- **How long it takes,** so clients can plan their day.
- **Your deposit and cancellation policy,** in plain words.

Prices that are clear before the appointment mean fewer surprises in the chair, and fewer bad reviews.

## Your address: salon, suite, home or mobile

Where you braid changes how your profile should look.

- **A salon or a suite with your name on it:** Google expects any business that shows its address to have [a permanent sign with its name at that address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en). Show the address, with the suite number.
- **Braiders working inside someone else's salon:** Google allows a profile for an individual practitioner with their own clients, named with just their name, separate from the salon's own profile.
- **If you go to clients' homes:** hide the address and set a [service area](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) instead (up to 20 cities or ZIP codes).
- **If you braid at home:** think it through. Google's rule about a sign applies to any address you show, and many braiders would rather not publish their home address. If you don't publish it, say clearly how clients get it, for example in the booking confirmation. When I asked ChatGPT, its first pick was a braider whose "exact street address isn't publicly confirmed": still first, but with a doubt attached.

## Hours and booking

- **Hours:** if you have set hours when clients can come or book, list them, and set [special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) for holidays. Google's guidelines say businesses that work **only by appointment** shouldn't list regular hours.
- **The Schedule button:** every salon in the map list I saw had one. To get booking on your profile, [connect a booking provider](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7475773?hl=en) (Business Profile → Bookings → choose your provider); Google says it shows up within a week. If you don't use one, you can add your own booking link.
- **Booking doesn't have to cost a monthly fee.** For Isa's salon (below), I set up a simple booking request form that sends her a message for each request.

## Photos of your work

Clients book braids with their eyes. Google asks for photos that are [in focus, well lit, and not heavily filtered](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en).

- Each style you want to be booked for, finished, from the front, back and side.
- Close-ups of parts and edges: when ChatGPT described one salon, it was "customers praise clean parts and consistent braiding".
- Your chair, your space and the outside, so clients know where they're going.
- New photos every week you can.

**Always ask your client's permission** before posting a photo of them, and crop out faces if they prefer.

## Category and name

- **Category:** every salon in the lists I saw used "Hair salon". Pick the most specific category the dashboard offers for what you do, and add others only if they're true. Google asks for [as few categories as possible, as specific as possible](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).
- **Name:** exactly as it appears on your sign, your Instagram and your website. Adding "Best Knotless Braids Cheap" to the name breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
- **Licence:** if your state requires one for braiding, show it on your website. Clients ask: "licensed braiders" came up in local Facebook groups.

## Reviews: clients describe what matters

In [BrightLocal's 2026 survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 US adults, about local businesses in general, 74% look for reviews from the last three months, 68% only choose businesses with four stars or more, and 89% expect the owner to reply.

Clients' own words decide a lot here. ChatGPT summed up salons from their reviews: "gentle braiding, patience and affordability". Among the things it asked a client to choose from was "gentle braiding (not too tight)". Google showed lines like "very calm and quite quick with braiding".

**How to ask.** When the client is happy in the mirror, text your Google review link or show a QR code. Ask every client.

**What not to do.**

- [Google's rules](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) forbid offering anything in exchange for a review, like a discount on the next set, and asking only the clients you know are happy.
- The FTC's [rule on reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) bans fake reviews (including AI-written ones), paying for positive reviews, and undisclosed reviews from people who work for you. It also limits owners asking their relatives for reviews. In a family salon, that matters.
- Never write reviews for clients, or tell them what to say.

**How to reply.** Short, personal, about their style. For a bad review: thank them, own what went wrong, say what you'll do, and offer to talk. Never share a client's details.

## Instagram, booking apps and everywhere else

- **Instagram:** braider accounts ranked on Google's first page, one with tens of thousands of followers and a booking link. Put the same name, phone and booking link on Instagram, your Google profile and your website, and link them to each other.
- **Booksy, StyleSeat and Yelp:** their pages ranked on the first page with prices and open appointments. If you use them, keep your prices and hours the same as on Google. Yelp says ChatGPT shows its [reviews, ratings, photos and business details](https://blog.yelp.com/news/yelp-chatgpt-integration/).
- **Apple Maps** is the maps app on every iPhone. [Apple Business Connect](https://businessconnect.apple.com/) is free and lets you control your listing.
- **Facebook groups:** local groups asking "who does affordable knotless braids?" rank high. Never post fake recommendations; happy clients will tag you.
- **AI answers:** in BrightLocal's survey, 45% had used ChatGPT or another AI tool to find a local business. ChatGPT also shows ads now: under my answer sat one for a hair product. Nobody can promise what an AI will say about you; make sure everything it reads is accurate, complete and the same everywhere.

## What it looked like for one salon

Isa's African Hair Braiding in Savannah, Georgia. Disclosure: Isa, the owner, is my cousin. Her Google profile had never been claimed. I claimed it, fixed the hours and address, added real photos, built a website with a page for each main style, and set up the free booking request form. The result: 450 calls from her Google profile in 3 months (to October 2026). Because the profile had never been claimed, there's no "before" number to compare with: this is a total, not a before-and-after. Her top search was simply "african hair braiding". The [full case study](/work/isa-african-hair-braiding/) has the details and sources.

## Your website, kept simple

You don't need a fancy site. You need one that answers what a client asks before booking, on a phone:

- a page for each style, with photos, a starting price, the time it takes and whether hair is included;
- a big "Book" button and a phone number people can tap;
- your deposit and cancellation policy;
- your address, or how clients get it;
- the style and your city in the page title, for example "Knotless braids in San Antonio".

## What takes an afternoon, and what takes months

In an afternoon: claim your profile, list your styles with prices, connect booking, upload photos of your work, and line up Instagram, Booksy or Yelp with the same details.

Over the following months: a review after every appointment, a reply to each one, new photos every week, and clients tagging you where others ask for a braider. Google takes time to reflect this. It usually takes months, and nobody can honestly guarantee where you'll rank.
